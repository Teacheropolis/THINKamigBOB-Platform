Paper Bridge Design Challenge

Grade 4 STEM · Formative assessment · Approximately 45 minutes

Ohio standards
Grade 4 Physical Science: investigate energy transfer and explain changes in a system using evidence.
Innovative Designer, Grades 3–5: generate ideas, test solutions, and improve artifacts.
Student-centered task
Teams design, build, test, and revise a paper bridge spanning 20 centimeters while choosing between two structural approaches.

Learning Objective and Success Criteria

Objective: Using the provided paper and fastening materials, students will design, test, and revise a bridge spanning 20 centimeters, record load-test data, and use the evidence to explain how one design feature affected performance.

  • Produce a labeled design before building.
  • Build a bridge that spans 20 centimeters.
  • Record the number of washers supported during each test.
  • Explain one cause-and-effect relationship using test evidence.
  • Make and justify one evidence-based revision.
Cognitive rigor: Students analyze test evidence, defend a design decision, revise a solution, and transfer their learning by proposing a design for a different span or material limit. This deliberately moves from DOK 2 application to DOK 3 strategic reasoning.

Materials, Resources, and Alternatives

Per team
  • Two sheets of paper
  • 30-centimeter ruler
  • 30 centimeters of tape
  • Two supports placed 20 centimeters apart
  • Washers and a collection cup
  • Design-and-data recording sheet
Alternatives and technology purpose
  • Books may replace bridge supports.
  • Coins or identical blocks may replace washers.
  • Printed picture directions replace the classroom display.
  • A visual timer supports pacing; it does not replace hands-on testing.

Instructional Sequence and Pacing

  1. Do Now — 5 minutes: Predict which paper shape will hold the greatest load and explain why.
  2. Plan — 7 minutes: Review the goal and constraints. Teams choose a structural approach, label a sketch, and assign roles.
  3. Build and first test — 18 minutes: Build, measure the span, add washers one at a time, and record the result.
  4. Analyze and revise — 10 minutes: Identify the design feature most connected to the result, revise one feature, and test again.
  5. Exit Ticket — 5 minutes: Explain what changed, cite test data, and identify the next improvement.

UDL Access, Choice, and Differentiation

Representation

Use spoken and written directions, a 20-centimeter span diagram, visual examples, previewed vocabulary, and optional read-aloud support.

Action and expression

Students may explain through writing, a labeled diagram, a brief conference, or an audio response while still submitting test data.

Targeted support

Offer a span template, picture directions, a partial data table, flexible grouping, and sentence frames such as “When we changed ___, the bridge held ___ because ___.”

Extension

Compare two designs or optimize the bridge under a stricter material limit, then defend the tradeoff.

Anticipated Challenges and Responsive Moves

Span is confused with total bridge length.
Model the distance between supports and have students verify it before building.
Several variables change at once.
Require teams to circle the single feature they will revise.
A conclusion is not supported by evidence.
Prompt: “Which test number proves that?” and provide the cause/effect frame.
Students finish early.
Assign the transfer constraint and require comparison of both designs.

Assessment, Alignment, and Next Instruction

Evidence reviewed: labeled sketch, prototype, span measurement, load-test table, observation notes, revision, and Exit Ticket/Captain’s Log response.

Mastery tool: Use the five success-criteria checks above. Four or five checks, including the evidence-based explanation, indicates mastery. Fewer than four identifies the exact criterion for reteaching.

  • If measurement is inaccurate: reteach span and measuring with a guided example.
  • If testing is inconsistent: model fair testing and controlled variables.
  • If the explanation lacks evidence: use a teacher conference and sentence frame.
  • If all criteria are met: assign the transfer constraint or comparison extension.
