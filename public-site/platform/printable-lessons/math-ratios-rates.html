Which Drink Mixture Is Strongest?

Grade 6 Mathematics · Formative assessment · Approximately 40 minutes

Ohio standard
6.RP.A.3: use ratio and rate reasoning to solve real-world problems, including tables, diagrams, double number lines, and equations.
Student-centered task
Partners compare drink-mixture recipes and choose a table, double number line, diagram, or manipulatives to represent and defend their reasoning.

Learning Objective and Success Criteria

Objective: Given two drink-mixture recipes and a choice of representation, students will calculate equivalent ratios or unit rates, identify the stronger mixture, and justify their conclusion with at least two accurate pieces of mathematical evidence.

  • Represent each ratio accurately.
  • Generate an equivalent ratio or calculate a correct unit rate.
  • Compare both recipes using corresponding quantities.
  • State the correct conclusion.
  • Justify the conclusion with two pieces of mathematical evidence.
Cognitive rigor: Students select a representation, analyze and critique comparisons, justify a conclusion, correct flawed reasoning, and transfer the strategy to a new recipe. This moves from DOK 2 application to DOK 3 strategic reasoning.

Materials, Resources, and Alternatives

Materials
  • Drink-recipe cards
  • Ratio-table and double-number-line templates
  • Measuring-cup visuals or manipulatives
  • Pencils and individual transfer problem
  • Exit Ticket/Captain’s Log
Alternatives and technology purpose
  • Printed cards replace projected recipes.
  • Colored counters replace measuring visuals.
  • Calculator access reduces computation barriers when reasoning is the target.
  • Audio recording allows explanation without lowering mathematical expectations.

Instructional Sequence and Pacing

  1. Do Now — 5 minutes: Compare two simple mixtures and record an initial claim.
  2. Model — 8 minutes: Demonstrate corresponding quantities and one representation without completing the class problem.
  3. Partner investigation — 12 minutes: Choose a representation, calculate, compare, and prepare a justification.
  4. Individual transfer — 10 minutes: Solve a new comparison independently and critique one incorrect solution.
  5. Exit Ticket — 5 minutes: Solve and explain, record an audio explanation, or correct an incorrect ratio table and explain the correction.

UDL Access, Choice, and Differentiation

Representation

Use recipe cards, ratio tables, double number lines, diagrams, manipulatives, vocabulary supports, a worked example, and read-aloud directions.

Action and expression

Students may use calculations, a table, a diagram, manipulatives, spoken reasoning, or writing, while all students provide mathematical evidence.

Targeted support

Color-code corresponding quantities, provide a partially completed model, use flexible grouping, and offer frames such as “For every ___, recipe A has ___ while recipe B has ___.”

Extension

Create an equivalent recipe under a new constraint and defend why its flavor strength is preserved.

Anticipated Challenges and Responsive Moves

Only one quantity is compared.
Ask students to identify both corresponding quantities before calculating.
The ratio is reversed.
Require labels and consistent order in every representation.
Additive reasoning replaces multiplicative reasoning.
Use a scale-factor table and ask what multiplies both quantities.
A unit rate is calculated but not interpreted.
Require a sentence naming what the number means in the recipe context.

Assessment, Alignment, and Next Instruction

Evidence reviewed: partner representation, calculations, teacher conference, individual transfer problem, error analysis, and Exit Ticket/Captain’s Log response.

Mastery tool: Use the five success-criteria checks above. Four or five checks, including a correct comparison and evidence-based justification, indicates mastery.

  • If ratios are reversed: reteach labeling and order with manipulatives.
  • If additive reasoning appears: use a guided scale-factor table.
  • If computation is correct but explanation is weak: conference with an evidence frame.
  • If all criteria are met: assign the constrained-recipe extension and peer critique.
